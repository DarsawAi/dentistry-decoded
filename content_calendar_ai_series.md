## Week 5: Endodontic Outcome Predictions (Reality Check)
- **Paper:** *Diagnostic Efficacy of Artificial Intelligence Models for Predicting Endodontic Outcome: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis* (Indian Journal of Dental Research, 2025)
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.4103/ijdr.ijdr_497_25
- **Asset:** `assets/ai_endodontics_1786433881469.jpg`
- **Facebook Caption:**
  > A new 2025 meta-analysis indicates that while AI models are widely used for identifying root structures, their pooled accuracy for predicting final endodontic outcomes remains highly variable. Researchers point out that structural limitations in current data models require clinical endodontic treatments to still rely heavily on standard specialist verification. 🦷🔍🔬
- **Facebook First Comment:**
  > This meta-analysis highlights a critical disconnect in dental AI research: while individual lab models boast high training accuracy, their real-world predictive validity remains strictly exploratory. The pooled specificity of 0.33 is particularly concerning, indicating a high rate of false positives that could lead to unnecessary retreatment or extractions if clinically adopted without caution. The authors' discussion emphasizes that endodontic pathology involves complex, multi-factorial healing dynamics that simple radiographic pixel analysis cannot fully capture. They recommend that future research shift away from retrospective datasets toward prospective, multicenter trials with standardized input protocols, using AI strictly as a secondary diagnostic assistant rather than an independent prognostic tool.
  > Full paper: https://doi.org/10.4103/ijdr.ijdr_497_25

## Week 8: The Grand Finale – Is Dentistry AI-Proof?

- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/00220345251356408
- **Asset:** `assets/ai_dentistry_proof_1786436074432.jpg`
- **Facebook Caption:**
  > A consensus review in the Journal of Dental Research emphasizes that while generative AI serves as a powerful diagnostic co-pilot, dentistry remains fundamentally "AI-proof." Because software lacks clinical empathy, tactile psychomotor skills, and nuanced ethical accountability, the final therapeutic decisions will always belong to human hands. 🦷🤝💻
- **Facebook First Comment:**
  > This consensus review serves as the definitive anchor for our "Is Dentistry AI-Proof?" series, illustrating that AI is a clinical augmenter, not a replacement. Despite the massive capabilities of large language and imaging models, the study highlights severe vulnerabilities like "automation bias"—where dentists might override their own training to trust flawed software predictions. The recommendations are clear: we must teach AI literacy in dental schools and treat AI outputs as mere suggestions. True clinical practice requires patient rapport, psychomotor dexterity during surgery, and final ethical accountability—none of which can be coded. Ultimately, the dentist's human touch remains entirely AI-proof.
